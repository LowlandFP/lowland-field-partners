International B2B Procurement Checklist

Before contacting manufacturers or sourcing agents, prepare a clear, comparable purchasing brief. This checklist is useful for machinery, food processing, solar operations, refrigeration, automation and business software.

1. Describe the commercial requirement

  • Legal company or organisation name, country and delivery location
  • Intended application and whether the requirement is active or exploratory
  • Approximate order quantity, capacity, throughput or user count
  • Target decision date and desired delivery or implementation window
  • Indicative budget range, if available, and currency

2. Specify technical and operational needs

  • Required performance, dimensions, interfaces, energy supply and operating environment
  • Applicable safety, regulatory, food-contact, electrical or cybersecurity requirements to verify with qualified parties
  • Existing systems, spare parts, training, language and local service needs
  • For SaaS: data hosting, security requirements, integration, seats and support needs

3. Compare supplier proposals consistently

  • Exact legal contracting entity and manufacturer identity
  • Scope of supply, exclusions, optional components and warranty terms
  • Delivery terms (including agreed Incoterms where relevant), lead time, import duties and taxes
  • Installation, commissioning, maintenance and after-sales responsibility
  • Payment milestones, acceptance tests, dispute resolution and documentation

4. Control disclosure and referral risk

  • Share only non-confidential requirements at first contact
  • Get consent before passing personal contact details or sensitive project information to third parties
  • Ask any intermediary to clarify whether a supplier pays a referral fee and whether that affects buyer pricing
  • Do not treat an introduction as proof of supplier suitability; independently perform technical, legal and financial due diligence
